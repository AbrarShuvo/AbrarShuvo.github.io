The problem

Buyers need to trust you before they'll ask the price.

Sourcing is a business built on trust: a buyer is sending money overseas for goods they haven't seen. The old setup gave them nothing to judge. Meanwhile every product change meant messaging a developer and waiting.

What the client needed
A catalog that looks credible to overseas buyers
Quotes by request, prices never shown publicly
Full control of content without touching code
An enquiry inbox that doesn't fill with spam
The approach

Design it, agree it, then build it

1

Every screen in Figma first

The client approved each page as a design, including the admin screens. Changing a picture is cheap; changing a finished site is not.

2

A deliberately small version one

No cart, no checkout, no accounts. Quotation only, so the first release ships and earns before any of that is paid for.

3

The client owns everything

The hosting and database accounts are in the client's name. If they ever stop working with me, nothing of theirs is locked up with me.

The admin dashboard

The part that decides whether a site stays alive

Most small business sites go stale because updating them is somebody else's job. Here the owner signs in and edits products, photos, prices, services and shipping information directly. Enquiries arrive in the same place.

Keeping the inbox clean

A quote form is a spam magnet

Any public form on a B2B site gets found by bots within days. If the owner stops trusting the enquiry inbox, the whole site stops working. Three quiet layers keep it usable.

A human checkInvisible to real buyers, a wall to scripts.
A limit on how oftenOne sender can't fire hundreds of enquiries at the server.
A hidden trap fieldPeople never see it. Bots fill it in and give themselves away.
